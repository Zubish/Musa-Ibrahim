Civic reporting products need credibility before they need decoration. If a user is submitting evidence, reading a live report, or reviewing a verification queue, the interface has to make source, status, and next action clear.

VisionEcho helped me think about mobile-first public-interest workflows. A reporter may capture evidence on a phone, an editor may need to review submissions quickly, and a reader may need enough context to trust what they are seeing.

Frontend design matters here because the product has to separate raw reports from verified information without making the interface heavy. Good filters, labels, readable cards, and accessible form states can make the difference between useful civic tech and a feed that feels chaotic.

I want this kind of writing to sit beside my project work because it shows the thinking behind the screen: frontend development is also about information quality, trust signals, and responsible interaction design.
