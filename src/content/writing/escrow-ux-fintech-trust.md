FinTech interfaces carry a different kind of pressure because users are not only clicking buttons; they are trusting the product with money. In an escrow product, unclear wording or weak state design can make a buyer or seller feel exposed.

BlackCrow pushed me to think about the transaction as a sequence of visible states: create escrow link, verify access, confirm payment boundary, track delivery, release funds, and handle withdrawal. Each step needs to tell the user what has happened, what is still pending, and who controls the next action.

Good frontend work in this space is partly visual hierarchy and partly product reasoning. The interface should not make the user decode the business logic. It should turn the logic into labels, disabled states, confirmation moments, and screens that reduce doubt.

That is the kind of FinTech UX I want to keep practicing: not flashy dashboards, but trustworthy flows that make sensitive actions feel understandable.
